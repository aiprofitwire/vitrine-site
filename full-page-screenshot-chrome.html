How to take a full page screenshot in Chrome

Checked November 4, 2026. Written for Vitrine 1.2.2.

Chrome has no “full page” button, but it can do it. There are three ways: a hidden command in DevTools (free, nothing to install), Print to PDF, and an extension. Here is each one, step by step, and when it is the right pick.

Way 1: Chrome’s built-in DevTools command

This works on Windows, Mac and Linux, with no install.

  1. Open the page. Scroll to the bottom once, so lazy images have loaded.
  2. Open DevTools: press F12 (Windows) or Cmd + Option + I (Mac). Right-click, then Inspect, also works.
  3. Open the Command Menu: Ctrl + Shift + P (Windows) or Cmd + Shift + P (Mac).
  4. Type “screenshot” and choose Capture full size screenshot.
  5. Chrome saves a PNG in your Downloads folder (or asks where, if you turned that on).

That is the whole trick. It is free and it is reliable on ordinary articles and product pages.

What it does not do: it takes the page exactly as it is. A cookie banner, a pop-up or an ad on the page is in the picture. Pages that scroll inside a box, like many inboxes and dashboards, may come back as one screen. Very long pages are where it is least reliable, so check the bottom of the image. The file is a bare PNG: no frame, no source link, no date.

Way 2: Print to PDF

Press Ctrl + P (Windows) or Cmd + P (Mac), set the destination to Save as PDF, and save. You get a paginated document with real, selectable text. It is not an image of the page as you see it: the page is cut into sheets and many sites change their layout for print. It suits articles you want to read or file. We cover it in full on full page screenshot to PDF.

Way 3: A one-key extension (Vitrine)

An extension does the scrolling for you. Vitrine is a free Chrome extension built for this.

  1. Add Vitrine from the Chrome Web Store. Click the puzzle piece in Chrome’s toolbar and pin it.
  2. Open the page you want.
  3. Press Alt + Shift + S (Windows) or Option + Shift + S (Mac). You can also click the Vitrine icon and choose Full page.
  4. Vitrine scrolls the page for you, loads the lazy images first, and shows sticky headers once instead of on every screen.
  5. The Studio opens with your capture. Click Copy to paste it straight into Slack, email or Notion, or Download as PNG, JPG or PDF.

What Vitrine adds to the plain capture

  • Cookie banners, chat bubbles, pop-ups and ads are hidden before the shot and put back after. Nothing is blocked on the page itself. Both switches are on by default.
  • Pages that scroll inside a box (inboxes, dashboards) are captured whole.
  • Very long pages are kept sharp. On a page too tall for one sharp image, Vitrine keeps sharp copies as separate pages, to save as a ZIP or a PDF, next to a scaled-down full image. Press Esc on a very long page to stop and keep what you have.
  • Three more ways to capture: visible area (Alt + Shift + V), a region you drag over (Alt + Shift + R), and a single element, like a card or a chart (Alt + Shift + E). On a Mac, use Option instead of Alt. If a shortcut does nothing, another extension may own it: change it at chrome://extensions/shortcuts.
  • Emails and API keys can be blurred before the image is taken.
  • Your screenshots never leave your computer. There is no account and no tracking.

The Studio is where you make it presentable: a browser frame, a backdrop, arrows, boxes, numbered steps and blur.

Which way should you use?

  • One quick shot of a simple page, once in a while: DevTools. It is free and already in Chrome.
  • A document to read or file: Print to PDF.
  • Pages you capture often, pages with banners and ads, long pages, or shots you will show to someone: an extension.
